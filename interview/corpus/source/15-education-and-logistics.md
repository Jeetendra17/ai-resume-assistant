### Q15.8 — Would he do a take-home assignment or a technical test?

- **Difficulty:** recruiter
- **Tags:** interviews, take-home, process
- **Asked by:** Recruiter, Hiring Manager

**Answer.**

His willingness and availability for one are questions for him; the resume does not say.

What the record suggests about how he would approach one, which is the more useful half of the answer:

- **He would build the evaluation before the solution.** Every system he has built comes with a way to tell whether it works — an eval set,
  a must-refuse set, a probe. On a take-home that usually means a small labelled set and a number, rather than a demo that looks right on
  the three inputs he tried.
- **He would write down what is not done.** His site publishes its own roadmap of gaps. Expect a short "what I would do next" section rather
  than a submission that pretends to be complete.
- **He would explain the trade-offs.** The About page of this site is mostly decisions and their reasons. A take-home submission from him is
  likely to read the same way.

For an AI engineering role, a take-home that tests those things — "here is a small corpus, build retrieval over it and tell us how you know
it works" — would show his strengths better than an algorithm puzzle, and would also expose his gaps honestly, which is the point of a test.

A live alternative that tends to work well: ask him to walk through the Venera feature or this site's retrieval code and explain a decision
he would now make differently.

**Follow-up.** *"How long would a take-home take him?"* — Depends on the task. Ask him to estimate before starting; how he scopes it is itself
informative.

**Grounded in.** Eval-first methodology, published roadmap, About section decisions write-up
